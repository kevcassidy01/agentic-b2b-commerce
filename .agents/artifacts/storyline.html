01

FROM LOCAL KNOWLEDGE TO THE FIRST ORDER

Launch a new store

"I'm opening a store in ZIP code 30308. What should I stock?"

The buyer agent asks Coca-Cola's assortment agent to recommend a product mix using illustrative local sales history. Pricing and fulfillment agents turn it into a deliverable opening-stock quote.

  1. Discover: Share the ZIP code, cooler capacity, opening date and budget.
  2. Recommend: Local assortment insights explain the product mix and quantities.
  3. Negotiate: Buyer and seller agents exchange pricing proposals within their authority.
  4. Commit: Jordan approves the final quote; fulfillment confirms the first order.

Human control: the owner explicitly approves the purchase. No silent recurring commitment.

02

DELEGATED BUYING, EXPLICIT AUTHORITY

Delegate with confidence

"I need $2,400 of drinks for the weekend. Can we get 15% off?"

Alex's purchasing agent coordinates with Jordan's owner agent. Budget authority and seller discount authority are evaluated separately, with human review when either agent reaches its limit.

  1. Request: Alex's purchase exceeds the manager's $1,000 spending limit.
  2. Coordinate: The manager agent sends the request and business rationale to the owner agent.
  3. Negotiate: A seller human reviews the excessive discount request; the demo returns 10% off.
  4. Approve or escalate: The $2,160 total exceeds the owner agent's $2,000 cap. Jordan approves, rejects, or reduces it to $1,800 for delegated agent approval.

Human control: requests beyond delegated authority remain on hold. The approval trail stays in the conversation.

03

FROM PURCHASE PATTERNS TO TRUSTED AUTOPILOT

Let your orders learn

"You buy this mix every two weeks. Shall I prepare your next order?"

The buyer agent learns product combinations and cadence from simulated purchase history. Seller and fulfillment agents propose a specific transaction, then coordinate recurring replenishment if Jordan authorizes it.

  1. Learn: Identify a repeated product mix across six sample orders.
  2. Propose: Present a 40-case, $936 transaction with a delivery window.
  3. Authorize: Place a one-time order, skip it, or approve a two-week recurring schedule.
  4. Protect: Apply a $1,000 cap and escalate price increases or substitutions. Pause and resume inline.

Human control: recurring buying requires explicit consent, clear guardrails and accessible pause controls.

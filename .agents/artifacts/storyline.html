FROM LOCAL KNOWLEDGE TO THE FIRST ORDER

Launch a new store

Jordan: "I'm opening Midtown Market in ZIP 30308. Keep the opening assortment under $5,000."

The buyer agent coordinates discovery; the assortment agent explains a fictional neighborhood mix; pricing and fulfillment agents validate sample terms. Jordan chooses space, delivery tradeoffs and final authorization through staged inline buttons.

  1. Discover the footprint: "I open Friday, with an eight-door cooler and backroom space." Choose this 168-case plan or a compact four-door, 84-case plan. ZIP 30308, Friday opening and the $5,000 budget are fixed demo inputs; stock includes backroom inventory, not a guarantee of cooler fit.
  2. Explain the mix: The assortment agent returns illustrative unit shares: Original 36%, Zero Sugar 29%, Sprite 21%, Fanta 14%. Whole-case quantities are 60 / 48 / 36 / 24, or 30 / 24 / 18 / 12 for the compact footprint. Review the rationale or revisit capacity before requesting a quote.
  3. Validate the quote: Q-1042 totals $3,912, or $1,956 compact, before tax. Delivery is included Thursday, 8 am-12 pm, ahead of opening. The budget headroom is not an all-in tax guarantee. Prices and availability are simulated.
  4. Negotiate conditional terms: "Find a better price, without signing me up for recurring orders." Keep Thursday at list price, or accept a flexible Wednesday/Thursday 8 am-12 pm slot for 8% off, within seller-agent authority. The discounted subtotals are $3,599.04 or $1,799.52. Neither option requires future volume or recurring permission.
  5. Approve or decline: Choosing terms is not approval. Jordan explicitly approves the final one-time quote or declines without an order. Fulfillment returns fictional confirmation CC-1042 only after approval.
  6. Follow through: "What should my receiving team do?" Alex checks storage, counts by SKU and reports shortages; first-week sell-through informs a later replenishment request, never an automatic reorder.

Outcome: an owner-approved simulated opening order or a closed draft. No real stock reservation, carrier contact, purchase or hidden recurring commitment.

02

DELEGATED BUYING, EXPLICIT AUTHORITY

Delegate with confidence

Alex: "I need $2,400 of drinks for the weekend. Can we get 15% off?"

The manager agent supplies the rationale and draft; the owner agent checks buyer spending policy; the seller pricing agent checks discount policy independently. Simulated seller human review does not replace buyer approval.

  1. Request, not purchase: Alex proposes 100 cases: 60 Original and 40 Zero Sugar at $24 per case, for Friday delivery before the weekend rush. The $2,400 draft exceeds Alex's $1,000 limit and is held.
  2. Analyze both authorities: The owner agent has a $2,000 subtotal cap. Even 15% off would leave $2,040, above that cap. The seller agent has only 8% discount authority. Choose simulated seller human review or withdraw without submitting anything.
  3. Review seller terms: The seller human declines 15% and approves 10% for this one-time draft: $240 savings, $2,160 before tax, delivery included Friday 8 am-12 pm. This discount approval grants no permission to spend.
  4. Escalate to Jordan: "Approve the $2,160 demo purchase," "Reject it," or "Revalidate a reduced basket." The full request cannot pass through the owner agent's cap without human owner approval.
  5. Reduce and revalidate: The smaller basket has 44 Original and 40 Zero Sugar cases. The seller human separately revalidates 10% on $2,016, yielding $1,814.40. Jordan can submit it for owner-agent approval under the $2,000 cap or reject it. It still exceeds Alex's authority; splitting requests to evade limits is not allowed.
  6. Close the loop: Approved paths return demo confirmation CC-2048 and Alex's receiving follow-up. Alex monitors Original when reduced by 16 cases. Rejection notifies Alex, submits nothing to fulfillment and requires any future basket to be a new justified request. The approval trail distinguishes seller approval from buyer approval.

Outcome: human approval of $2,160, delegated owner-agent approval of a revalidated $1,814.40 basket, or no order. Tax is excluded. Approval history exists only in this session, not a persisted audit system.

03

FROM PURCHASE PATTERNS TO TRUSTED AUTOPILOT

Let your orders learn

Jordan: "What should I order next based on my recent purchases?"

The buyer agent explains six fictional purchase baskets. The assortment agent preserves the familiar mix; seller pricing and fulfillment agents return a concrete quote. Purchase history suggests a starting point, not measured sell-through or permission to buy.

  1. Explain the evidence: Roughly every 14 days, the mock account buys Original 16 cases, Zero Sugar 12, Sprite 8 and Fanta 4. Original supports volume, Zero Sugar keeps a no-sugar option, and smaller flavor lines preserve variety. Jordan should verify on-hand stock.
  2. Choose and price: "Price the familiar basket" gives 40 cases for $936; "I have stock left" halves every line to 20 cases for $468. Both are before tax with delivery included next Tuesday, 8 am-12 pm. Approve a one-time demo order, skip, adjust, or review recurring guards.
  3. Adjust the draft: Inline choices offer 40 cases every 14 days, 20 every 14 days, or 40 every 21 days. Longer cadence changes frequency, not unit price. Selecting an option updates the proposal but authorizes neither purchase nor schedule.
  4. Review explicit recurring permission: AUTO-301 names the chosen basket, cadence and location, with a $1,000 subtotal cap and 48-hour notification. Any price increase, substitution, quantity change, delivery change or cap breach holds the occurrence for owner approval. Tax is not covered by that cap; real deployment would need an all-in approval policy. No minimum term or cancellation fee applies in this demo.
  5. Authorize or decline: "Authorize this demo schedule" activates only the in-session simulation. "Not now" closes it with no order. A one-time approval confirms CC-3001 but never creates recurring permission.
  6. Exercise an exception: Simulate a 5% price increase: $982.80 for 40 cases or $491.40 for 20. Even below the cap, the increase is held. Approve only that occurrence, skip it, or cancel. Occurrence approval never raises the future price baseline; silence never means consent.
  7. Control the lifecycle: Pause prevents planned submissions; resume revalidates the same basket and guards for the next Tuesday window, with no catch-up orders. Cancel from active, paused or exception review to remove future occurrences. A cancelled schedule cannot resume; new permission is required. Cancellation does not undo a separately approved demo occurrence.

Outcome: a one-time demo confirmation, skipped recommendation, or explicitly controlled demo schedule. No real learning service, background job, persistent schedule or purchase exists.
